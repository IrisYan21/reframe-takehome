# Pass 3 · What 836 Reddit comments say about the people onboarding loses

*73 threads · r/stopdrinking, r/dryalcoholics, r/SoberCurious*

## 1 · What they care about

| They care about | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **Feeling better, not being fixed** | Sleep in 282 sentences, anxiety in 106. "my sleep is always garbage if I drink" |
| **Not needing a label to qualify** | 94 identity sentences. "I hate the term alcoholic" |
| **A goal they can change** | "moderation mode… 30 drinks to 12… stopped fully in February" |
| **Seeing progress add up** | "money saved, calories avoided… numbers going up are motivating" |

## 2 · What drives them away

1. **Price against free rivals.** I Am Sober named 31×, Reframe 23×. "prohibitively pricey"
2. **Bundled features they never wanted.** "can't stand the meetings… isn't worth the subscription"
3. **Effort to start and to fix a slip.** "I don't need to spend 5 to 10 minutes to log it"
4. **Account and data demands.** "I really value not needing an account"
5. **Losing their streak after a relapse.** "don't have to enter a sober date every Day 1, unlike Reframe"

## 3 · Dos and don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Open with what they've noticed: sleep, mood, mornings | Open with how much they drink |
| Ask for a goal in their words: cut back, dry month, quit | Ask them to accept a label |
| Let the goal change anytime | Lock a goal in as an identity |
| Show a personal result before the price | Hold value behind a 12-minute intake |
| Let them switch off community and meetings | Sell every feature as one bundle |
| Keep total days when they slip | Reset them to zero |

**Bottom line:** they arrive tired and unsure they belong. Onboarding wins by removing the need to prove they have a problem.
