# Target user profile — Reframe (pre-work for Pass 3)

**Purpose.** Set a shared picture of who Reframe's onboarding is written for, so Reddit mining in Pass 3 samples the right voices and codes them against the right needs. Desk research only, 2026-09-11. Age band 35–55 comes from the HR interview; everything else is public secondary evidence.

## Who they are

- **Peak-drinking age band, against a falling national trend.** 35–54 is the highest-drinking adult group in the US (~63% drink; Gallup 2025 puts overall adult drinking at a record-low 54%). Younger cohorts are quitting culturally; this band is the one still drinking and increasingly uneasy about it.
- **Majority women.** Roughly 60% of Reframe's users are women. Sober-coaching practices skew even harder — one reports 85% of clients are women aged 45–55.
- **Employed, functional, paying.** ~$99.99/yr with a 7-day trial filters to people with disposable income who see this as self-improvement spend, not treatment. Reframe reports ~3.2M downloads, ~2M active, ~150k paying — a low single-digit conversion, so onboarding is talking mostly to browsers.
- **Comfortable with apps, not credulous.** Gen X leads all generations in using apps to manage health conditions and has ~90% smartphone ownership, but about 27% are uncomfortable handing personal data to brands. They accept the value exchange only when the value is visible first.

## What brings them in

- **"Cut back," not "quit."** ~70% of Reframe users arrive wanting to moderate; only 30% arrive wanting abstinence. The product's default identity has to hold both without forcing a label.
- **Gray-area drinking is the self-description.** Not rock bottom, not take-it-or-leave-it. People in this band explicitly reject the alcoholic label and reject AA-shaped solutions, which is the whole reason an app exists in this gap.
- **Symptom-led triggers, not disease-led.** Sleep quality, morning anxiety, weight, blood pressure, a bad physical, energy. Alcohol is the suspected cause of a felt problem, rarely the stated identity problem.
- **Midlife load is the context.** Perimenopause and menopause, divorce, caregiving, financial stress, empty nest, career pressure. Drinking is the coping mechanism they want to replace, so removing it without offering a substitute reads as loss.
- **Socially normalized habit.** Risky drinking is socially acceptable in this cohort, particularly for women in midlife. Their environment does not support the change, so the app carries more of the motivational load than it would for a younger user.

## What this implies for onboarding

- **Shame is the primary failure mode.** Any question that reads as diagnosis ("are you dependent?") collides with the "I'm not an alcoholic" self-image and gives them a reason to close the app.
- **They pay for relief from a symptom, not a program.** Personalization is legible when it reflects back sleep, anxiety or energy — the thing they actually came for.
- **Effort tolerance is low and value proof must come early.** High-privacy-awareness, time-poor, mid-career users will give data, but only after the value exchange is obvious.
- **Non-judgmental and non-abstinence-first framing is the price of entry,** because most of them are not here to quit.

**Limitation.** No first-party analytics, and the 35–55 band is a single unverified HR claim. Public figures (60% women, 70/30 cut-back split) are company-reported and unaudited. Pass 3 tests whether Reddit voices actually match this profile before any of it is used as a finding.
